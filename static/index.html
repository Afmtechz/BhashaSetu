The lecture only reaches the students who understand it

Nearly 40% of children worldwide start school in a language different from the one spoken at home, according to UNESCO. In Indian classrooms, that mismatch shows up as lower comprehension, less participation, and higher dropout, even when the teaching itself is strong. NEP 2020 asks for mother-tongue instruction; most classrooms don't have the staff to deliver a lecture in five languages at once.

How it works

One lecture, delivered in whatever language each student needs, without the teacher changing how they teach.

01

Teacher speaks

The teacher talks normally, in their own language, code-mixing included.

02

Speech is recognised

Speech-to-text transcribes each sentence in real time as it's spoken.

03

Meaning is translated

Each sentence is translated into every language in the room, keeping technical terms accurate.

04

Students receive it

Subtitles and natural spoken audio reach each student's device, under two seconds behind.

What the platform does

Built around one classroom session, from the first word spoken to the notes a student reviews afterward.

Real-time speech capture

Picks up the teacher's voice continuously through the class device, no extra hardware.

Live subtitles

Timestamped captions appear in each student's chosen language as the lecture happens.

Natural voice output

Translated audio, not a flat text-to-speech read, so it's easier to follow by ear.

AI-generated notes and quizzes

Once class ends, students get a short summary and a few review questions in their own language.

Multilingual classroom mode

One session, many students, each one hearing and reading a different language at once.

Works offline

Local caching keeps a lecture running through a dropped connection, then syncs when it's back.

Designed for schools with real connectivity constraints

Rural institutions are often where the language barrier is widest and the network is weakest. Bhasha-setu AI is built to keep working through both: lectures cache locally and sync when connectivity returns, so a dropped connection doesn't mean a lost lecture.

The initial rollout targets English and Telugu, including code-mixed speech, with the architecture planned to extend across all 22 scheduled Indian languages.
